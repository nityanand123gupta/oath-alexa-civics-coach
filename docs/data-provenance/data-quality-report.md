## Transcription decisions and uncertainties

1. **Bullet symbols stripped; whitespace normalized.** PDF line wraps were joined and runs of spaces/tabs collapsed to single spaces. The PDFs' typographic punctuation (curly apostrophes ' and curly quotes " ") is preserved as printed. A pypdf extraction artifact ("state' s" with an inserted space) was corrected to the printed "state's" (affects 2008 Q25/Q42/Q68/Q100 extraction only; the PDFs print normal possessives).
2. **Statue of Liberty "Also acceptable" note (2008 Q95, 2025 Q120).** USCIS prints two bullets ("New York (Harbor)", "Liberty Island") plus the note "[Also acceptable are New Jersey, near New York City, and on the Hudson (River).]". Because USCIS declares these "also acceptable," they were split into three additional answer strings: "New Jersey", "near New York City", "on the Hudson (River)". If you prefer strict bullet-only fidelity, remove the last three answers and keep the note.
3. **American Indian tribe questions.** 2008 Q87 carries the official note "[USCIS Officers will be supplied with a list of federally recognized American Indian tribes.]" and 2025 Q117 carries "For a complete list of tribes, please visit bia.gov." The schema has no note field for non-dynamic questions, so these notes are recorded here only. The listed example tribes (22 in 2008, 25 in 2025) are transcribed as the acceptable answers, but note USCIS accepts any federally recognized tribe.
4. **Dynamic questions' acceptableAnswers** contain the official placeholder text exactly as USCIS lists it (e.g., "Visit uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates for the name of the President of the United States."), duplicated in `dynamicNote`. A consuming product must resolve current officeholder/state answers at runtime; USCIS "will not accept an incorrect answer."
5. **Category names** were title-cased from the PDFs' ALL-CAPS section headings ("AMERICAN GOVERNMENT" -> "American Government"); subcategory strings (e.g., "A: Principles of American Democracy") are verbatim. Note the 2008 test's category is "Integrated Civics" while 2025's third category is "Symbols and Holidays", and the 2008 subcategory is "A: Principles of American *Democracy*" vs. 2025 "A: Principles of American *Government*" — these differences are in the sources, not errors.
6. **Known 2025-vs-2020 content differences observed** (confirming we transcribed the 2025 document, not the 2020 one): 2025 Q48 lists "Secretary of War (Defense)" (2020 had "Secretary of Defense") and "Vice-President" (hyphenated as printed); 2025 Q126 includes "Juneteenth" (absent from the 2020 65/20 doc's answer list).
7. **No uncertainties remain** about question/answer wording: every answer count was machine-verified against the source bullets and both documents extracted cleanly. The only judgment calls are items 2, 3, and 5 above.
